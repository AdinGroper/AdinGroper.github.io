Buenos Aires

August 2026

Buenos Aires is the capital of and largest city in Argentina. Considered an autonomous district, it is located within the Buenos Aires Province on the country's northeast coast. It has served as an important port city for trade and immigration within the Rio de la Plata region. Today, this city is known for its European-style architecture, lively neighborhoods, passionate people, and a strong culture of food and football.

My friend Brian was getting married in his hometown city of Buenos Aires, so when I received the wedding invitation, I knew I had to be there. All throughout college, I talked about how cool it would be to travel with him down to Argentina and see parts of the country. With his wedding date set, I now had the perfect excuse to take off from work and fly down to visit.


However, my trip was met with obstacles before it had even begun. Since there were no direct flights from Boston to Buenos Aires, I booked a connection in Atlanta, and was supposed to fly out Thursday night and arrive Friday morning. After leaving work Thursday and hurrying to the airport, I made it to Atlanta just fine, but my connecting flight was plagued by delays. The 11pm departure time kept getting pushed back an hour at a time while the plane was "undergoing maintenance", and by 1am, once they finally found an alternate plane to take us, the gate agent declared that the crew had timed out and we would all have to return back the next day for a 9am departure.


After much confusion, the airline finally issued hotel vouchers. I picked the first one on the list that had available rooms, around 4 miles from the airport, and quickly made my way over to the shuttle pick up area. There, we were told the free shuttles would only come once every half hour, and only have room for a dozen or so people. Hearing this, I banded together with a few other weary travelers and managed to hitch a ride with a taxi for only $5 a piece - a worthy cost for the time saved. The hotel itself was nothing special, and by the time I'd arrived to my room it was already almost 3am. Exhausted, I washed up and went right to bed, getting maybe a few hours of sleep before waking up at 6am and heading back over to the Atlanta airport.

The rest of the journey there went relatively smooth. Boarding was quick. I must've passed out in my seat before we even left the gate - I remember hearing the flight attendants start their safety speech, and the next thing I knew, we'd been flying for 2 hours. I've been told being able to sleep on planes is a superpower, and I'm sure glad to be blessed with such ability in my youth. For the rest of the 10-hour flight, I mostly watched movies and took the occasional nap. Immigration went fine, although it was disappointing they don't stamp the passports anymore. But before I knew it I was in the car with Brian and headed into the city.


At this point it was already after 9pm, and I'd lost the whole day Friday to the delay in Atlanta. But the night was young. Once in the city, we stopped at my Airbnb momentarily so I could drop off my luggage and shower real quick. Then we drove to Palermo, a lively neighborhood in the city with lots of nightlife, to meet up with his fianceé and her friends for dinner and drinks. Brian and I split a milanesa dish, which was thin breaded beef similar to a chicken cutlet, and man was this thing massive. I've eaten milanesa before in the States, but this one was certainly tasty. I also downed a couple glasses of Stella Artois. After dinner, we all went out to a bar for a little while, but it never picked up so we just called it a night.

Since I'd already lost a day of my trip, I got up early the next day to meet up with Brian and explore the city. Despite the late night prior, there was no time to waste. We met outside my Airbnb around 8am and took the closest "Subte" station straight downtown. After around 20 minutes or so, we had arrived at the Callao station and started walking south.

It was only a short walk until we reached our first point of interest: the National Congress building (Congreso Nacional) and the Plaza Congreso that stretched out in front of it. To the side was the National Senate building (Senado de la Nación), which had a much more discrete design compared to the congress building standing front and center. As we rounded the corner of the plaza, I caught a glimpse of some green animals out of the corner of my eye mixed in with a group of pigeons. At first I thought someone had just dyed the pigeons green... turns out, these were actually green parrots just out and about in the wild. Can't say I've seen that before, but was super cool!

Walking east from the plaza, we passed by the Barolo Palace, a one-hundred year old European-style building that stands out amongst the surrounding buildings. A tower and searchlight rise up from the center of the building, and its interior offers a flash back to the past with old-school elevators still in service.

Continuing east along Avenida de Mayo, we stopped into a cafe for a quick breakfast - consisting of a coffee and a medialuna, which was sweet crescent-shaped pastry similar to a French croissant. Eventually we stepped out onto the wide open Avenida 9 de Julio, one of the widest avenues in the world. Located at the intersection of Avenida 9 de Julio and two other major avenues was the Plaza de la República, and from the center rose the iconic Obelisco de Buenos Aires, a white obelisk standing at over 200 feet tall.

Just up the street, on the western side, was the Teatro Colón, a historic opera house/theatre built over one-hundred years ago. I unfortunately did not get to go inside, but Teatro Colón is ranked as one of the best opera houses in the world. Behind the theatre was a large park with some monuments and walking paths, and several other European-style buildings including a fancy-looking school and the Argentina Supreme Court (Palacio de Justicia de la Nación).

From there, we walked back east into the Retiro neighborhood, passing by the famous Galerías Pacífico shopping center, and north up into the Plaza San Martin. Across the street, at the far end of the plaza, was a stone and brick clock tower called Torre Monumental, or as Brian called it, the "English Tower". It rose up over the trees and greenery of the Plaza San Martin, offering a nice view on the horizon.

Staying along the coast, we went south to the Plaza de Mayo. There we saw the National Bank of Argentina and the "Pink House" (Casa Rosada), which is Argentina's equivalent of the White House in the USA. Similar to the White House, Casa Rosada serves as an important government building for the President, and is often a place for major demonstrations and/or protests (as evidenced by all of the military barricades placed out front). Of all the unique buildings I've seen so far in Buenos Aires, this one stands out as one of my favorites. Simple in concept yet very unique.

Just a bit east is the Puerto Madero waterfront neighborhood, our next stop of the day. There were a lot of restaurants and shops along the water, with the ARA Presidente Sarmiento tallship docked out front and the Puente de la Mujer (Bridge of the Mother) offering a way to cross to the business district on the other side. Unfortunately I didn't get a full picture of the bridge, but supposedly it pivots in the middle of the river to turn out of the way if a larger ship needs to pass through the waterway.

After a quick bus ride from Puerto Madero down to La Boca neighborhood, we explored the colorful Caminito alleyways. Here, there were colorful buildings all around us, with each side of the narrow roads lined with shops, restaurants, and market vendors. I ended up buying my Buenos Aires magnet here to add to my collection. There was also a mannequin of Lionel Messi raising up the world cup trophy in one of the windows which was pretty funny.

Nearby was the football (soccer) stadium La Bombonera, home of the Boca Juniors team - one of the two major teams in the city. The stadium itself, which I only got to see from the outside, was a D-shaped stadium packed into the middle of a neighborhood. It quite literally just appears in front of you as you round the corner of the street, the blue exterior rising above all of the surrounding blocks.

At this point in the day, it was just after noon and we were both getting hungry for lunch. So we ordered an Uber to the Chinatown district, Barrio Chino. The Chinatown in Buenos Aires was pretty cool, nothing like I'd ever seen before. It stretched along the length of an elevated train line for quite a few blocks, and was lined on either side with shops, restaurants, and cafes all offering Asian-style food. Typically the space below elevated car/train bridges is left empty in a city, so seeing how this space was transformed into such a bustling area of activity was a fresh sight. Brian and I both ended up ordering "sushi cups" at one of the food kiosks, which consisted of salmon, rice, avocado, and cheese piled into a plastic cup.

Our last stop of the day was just north of Barrio Chino, the stadium for the other major football/soccer team in Buenos Aires - River Plate. Again, only saw it from the outside, but unlike the Boca stadium, this one seemed much larger and much more out in the open.

In the afternoon, we went back to Brian's parents' house to chill for a bit. Here, they offered me the traditional Argentinian Mate drink and explained the customs behind it. There's a special cup and filtered straw you use to drink Mate. To prepare it, you put the chopped leaves of the mate plant into the cup and then pour hot water over it. Typically friends or family will drink this as a social activity, passing around the cup with the leaves in it and one person in the group refilling the hot water from a flask after each person finishes their turn drinking. It had a bitter taste at first, which got a little better over time, but I can't say it was my favorite drink of all time. I'm sure if you grew up drinking it, or similar to beer, drink a lot of it, you eventually get used to the flavor. But overall a really cool experience; I'm glad his parents could share a part of their culture with me.

We spent a couple hours at his parents' apartment before they walked me back to my Airbnb for the evening. On the way, we stopped into a bakery and ordered a few variations of the sándwich de miga. At its core, this was just a sandwich of crustless white bread layered with different fillings, such as ham, cheese, egg, etc. But it's apparently a popular snacking food in Argentina so trying it was part of the cultural experience. I ordered the classic jamón y queso (ham and cheese), and Brian got a few with egg and cheese. The sandwich comes stacked in multiple layers, so it wasn't just bread-filling-bread, rather, bread-filling-bread-filling-bread, which was interesting. And overall, it tasted pretty good. I can see why this is considered a popular snack for so many.

I rested for an hour or so before meeting back up with Brian and his parents for dinner - we planned to go to a nice steakhouse to enjoy the ever-so-popular Argentinian meats. Prior to eating, we drove around the nearby neighborhoods a bit, passing by large houses, small landmarks, and even a Statue of Liberty replica! We also stopped into a new Lucciano's ice cream/gelato shop that looked like a "Grand Hotel", as it was called, before arriving at a street lined with steakhouses.


We stopped into one on the corner, called La Azulada, and once we sat down, the food just kept coming. I tried two different types of sausage, three different cuts of steak, mashed potatoes, sweet potatoes, yuca, salad, and two different lemonades. It was a lot of food, and it all came out on one long wooden board. I did my best to try everything, but could only eat so much. Ending the day with such a nice dinner was definitely a treat. And to top it all off, we went to another Lucciano's for dessert after; I ordered a small cone of dubai chocolate gelato, and was served so much more than I'd originally wanted, but the chocolate tasted good so I managed to put it all down (despite making a bit of a mess in the process as it melted in my hands).


I finally got back to my Airbnb around midnight, and had planned to go see the Recoleta Cemetery the next morning, which we had missed. The cemetery is one of the most popular tourist attractions in the city, filled with many intricate mausoleums, including those of some well-known historical figures. We had missed it in our walking tour earlier that day, and it actually seemed like a cool spot to check out while I had some time to kill before the wedding Sunday. But after the craziness of the past 48 hours, I ended up killing that time in bed, sleeping in. I didn't actually wake up until after 1pm. I was a bit disappointed I didn't get to take advantage of one last opportunity to explore the city, but I knew the sleep would be much needed since I wouldn't get much rest after the wedding party.


Anyway, I quickly showered and got ready for the day. I tried to get a quick bite at the bakery across the street, the same place we'd ordered the sándwiches de miga the day prior, but it was really crowded and I wasn't entirely sure how to order, so I just went back to the Airbnb and changed into my suit.

The wedding itself went pretty well. I hitched a ride to the venue with Brian's (at the time) fianceé's friend, Pili, and her boyfriend Marcos, who were both super cool to talk to. The ceremony was set up just outside the venue, and though it had been overcast the entire day, the sun suddenly came out as Brian and Ailin walked down the aisle. It was such a surreal moment. I was also impressed with myself for understanding more than half of what was being said throughout the ceremony, since it was all in Spanish. I even got a shoutout from his dad for flying in all the way from the U.S.!

The party afterward was also a lot of fun. At first it was a bit awkward for me since I didn't know anyone and everyone spoke Spanish, but once I started to meet some of their family and friends, I felt a lot more comfortable. Everyone was really nice. I think I was definitely the token American in the room because a bunch of Brian's friends kept coming up to chat with me in English. The multi-course dinner was tasty. The drinks were great; I tried the Argentinian "national drink", fernet de coca, which consisted of Fernet-Branca liquor and Coca Cola mixed together. . Once I got into my groove for the night, I had a fun time dancing and celebrating with everyone, and before I knew it, the party was over.

I rode back with Brian's family, and didn't get back to my Airbnb until around 3 in the morning. Oh, and I forgot to mention, I had an 8am flight out the next day. Originally I thought I could stay up until I had to leave for the airport around 6, but after an hour scrolling on my phone at 4 in the morning, I decided to get a quick rest. I slept lightly for maybe an hour and a half before getting up to pack my things and Uber to the airport. Next stop: Lima, Peru.


I honestly managed to see and do a lot more than I had expected after losing a full day in Buenos Aires from my delayed departure flight. But after spending just 2 days in the city, I learned how much more there is to see and do in Argentina. It's such a large country, with so many different landscapes and places to visit. I definitely have to plan another trip, next time a longer trip, so I can experience more that this country has to offer. But I'm really glad I was able to make it down, if even only for a few days, to see some of Buenos Aires and celebrate my good friend's wedding day with everyone.
